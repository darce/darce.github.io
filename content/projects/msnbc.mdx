## Context

MSNBC.com had recently been rebuilt on Drupal following the Microsoft/NBC split. [Lullabot’s account](https://www.lullabot.com/articles/msnbc-video-and-drupalcon-case-study) describes the broader site as a ground-up rebuild that launched in October 2013. Once that site was live, the network needed a dedicated video experience.

The new surface had to support watching live and archived coverage while discovering what to watch next. Sending viewers away from the player to browse would break that continuity. The product problem was to make exploration and playback work together on the same page.

My project record describes a hard six-month deadline, with partner-litigation risk if the launch slipped. Working prototypes therefore had to move the team toward a production decision within a fixed delivery window.

## My role

I used functional prototypes to propose how the video experience should behave, then implemented front-end patterns for production. Doing both the design and the implementation meant I could carry a question into working software and refine it with the team.

I collaborated with MSNBC’s internal team, external vendors, and stakeholders as the prototypes evolved. I proposed and implemented interface patterns within that shared process; I did not independently own the entire product direction or the broader MSNBC.com rebuild. My remit covered the dedicated video experience and the AngularJS components I contributed to MSNBC’s in-house CMS, which I integrated with the Drupal backend powering the broader site.

## The video platform

### From concept to working prototypes

The initial concept centred on continuous viewing: a person should be able to watch coverage while looking through related stories and engagement features. I translated that goal into working prototypes, so the conversation could move from a proposed layout to how the interface behaved.

The prototypes went through multiple iterations with stakeholders. I then carried those proposals into production development, so the behaviour the prototypes settled is the behaviour that shipped.

### Collapsible panes around a running player

My proposal was to give the surrounding content its own collapsible panes. The player kept running while a viewer opened the discussion, scanned the latest coverage, or closed both to watch at full width. Choosing what to watch next never required leaving the page or interrupting the stream.

That put playback and conversation on screen at the same time. Comments usually lived below the fold or on a separate page, so a viewer who wanted to read them gave up the video to do it. Here they sat beside the player and could be dismissed. The arrangement is unremarkable on a video site today; in 2013 it was a proposal that needed a working prototype before the team would commit to it.

In live coverage that continuity mattered most, because the broadcast could move on while someone was choosing another story. An “Up next” row below the player brought the next viewing choice close to the current clip, and the adjacent video list provided a broader route through coverage.

The responsive layouts kept the player prominent as the available width changed. Desktop had room for horizontal video rows and a side panel; narrower layouts placed recommendations into vertical lists. The arrangement let browsing adapt to the screen while keeping the current video central.

### Through production to launch

The video platform launched on schedule within the six-month window. According to my project record, meeting that deadline helped avert the partner-litigation risk associated with a delayed launch.
