## A dedicated video experience after the site rebuild

MSNBC.com had recently been rebuilt on Drupal following the Microsoft/NBC split. [Lullabot’s account](https://www.lullabot.com/articles/msnbc-video-and-drupalcon-case-study) describes the broader site as a ground-up rebuild that launched in October 2013. Once that site was live, the network needed a dedicated video experience.

The new surface had to support watching live and archived coverage while discovering what to watch next. Browsing needed to stay on the viewing page so the stream could continue while someone chose what to watch next.

My project record describes a hard six-month deadline, with partner-litigation risk if the launch slipped. The prototypes had to settle interface behaviour in time for production development.

## My role

I proposed interface behaviour in functional prototypes and implemented front-end patterns for production. The prototypes gave the team a way to try a proposal before deciding how to build it.

I worked with MSNBC’s internal team, external vendors, and stakeholders. My remit covered the dedicated video experience and the AngularJS components I contributed to the in-house CMS, integrated with the Drupal backend. The broader MSNBC.com redesign was a separate team effort.

## The video platform

### From concept to working prototypes

I prototyped ways to browse coverage and comments around a running player. After several iterations with stakeholders, I helped carry the agreed interactions into the production AngularJS interface.

### Collapsible panels around a running player

My proposal was to give the surrounding content its own collapsible panels. The player kept running while a viewer opened the discussion, scanned the latest coverage, or closed both to watch at full width. Choosing what to watch next never required leaving the page or interrupting the stream.

Comments sat beside the player and could be dismissed. The prototype made that arrangement testable within the page, including what happened to the player’s width as panels opened and closed.

In live coverage that continuity mattered most, because the broadcast could move on while someone was choosing another story. An “Up next” row below the player brought the next viewing choice close to the current clip, and the adjacent video list provided a broader route through coverage.

The responsive layouts kept the player prominent as the available width changed. Desktop had room for horizontal video rows and a side panel; narrower layouts placed recommendations into vertical lists. The arrangement let browsing adapt to the screen while keeping the current video central.

### Through production to launch

The video platform launched on schedule within the six-month window. According to my project record, meeting that deadline helped avert the partner-litigation risk associated with a delayed launch.
