</Intro>

### What a prototype settles

My background is in interactive media and front-end development. That experience lets me turn a proposed interaction into something a team can try, question, and refine. A working prototype shows what an interaction costs to build and how much work it leaves to the person using it. A description of the interaction settles neither.

### Two products of my own

Both show a person what a model made of a picture. [Visual Search Workbench](/projects/visual-search-workbench/) searches the photo folders you choose, by words or by a reference picture, so you can begin with an image you like and follow the collection toward an idea you cannot yet name. [AltContext](/projects/altcontext/) matches faces against a roster the site has confirmed, so a description can name who is at the lectern rather than report an anonymous figure. That gives blind readers the recognition a sighted reader gets in an instant, and it gives every description on the site a name that has been checked. In each, the software proposes and the person accepts, changes, or refuses.

### Bringing the work to launch

At [PhotoShelter](/projects/photoshelter/), I combined product R&D with accessibility work on a large digital-asset-management platform. A workflow prototype exposed integration problems before production development, while my WCAG audit and remediation work helped address the requirements of enterprise customers. The case study gives the figures and the evidence behind them.

At [MSNBC](/projects/msnbc/), I helped take a dedicated video experience from product definition to launch in six months. I proposed collapsible panes that kept the player, the conversation around it, and the rest of the coverage on one screen, so a viewer could follow comments and choose the next clip without stopping playback. That layout is ordinary now. It was not in 2013, when I proposed it.

### How I decide

I write the constraints down before the build starts. Accessibility enters the plan alongside the feature, not as an audit afterwards. Deterministic tests go in at the same time, and they steer what gets built next: a check I cannot write yet marks a feature I have not defined yet.

I maintain [Heuristics Canon](/research/heuristics-canon/), a set of rules tied to published sources, so a decision can point at a rule and at the book behind it. [WorkBay](/projects/workbay/) puts that prior work in front of the coding agents I direct, so an implementation starts from what the project already settled rather than from a blank page.
