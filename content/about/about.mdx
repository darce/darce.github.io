<Intro>

<h2>Hello, I'm Daniel Arcé.</h2>

I'm a product engineer. I work on products where the interaction model and the technical model are hard to separate: AI-assisted tools, data-heavy interfaces, accessibility-critical workflows, and prototypes that need to survive contact with real system constraints.

</Intro>

<CardGrid>

<AboutCard label="Background" title="Interactive media, then front-end systems">

I started in interactive media and design technology, then spent much of my career building front-end systems for media companies, consumer products, and SaaS teams. That engineering depth changed how I design: I can test an interaction against the actual data model, browser or platform behavior, accessibility tree, and failure states before a handoff turns those constraints into surprises.

</AboutCard>

<AboutCard label="Evidence" title="PhotoShelter">

At PhotoShelter I combined product R&D with accessibility work on a digital-asset-management platform. I led the WCAG 2.1 audit and remediation for contracts that had to demonstrate compliance, and a workflow-automation prototype surfaced integration problems early enough to cut the later implementation work by an internal estimate of roughly 30%. The [case](/projects/photoshelter/) carries the figures and where they come from.

</AboutCard>

<AboutCard label="Now" title="Visual Search Workbench and WorkBay">

My recent independent work moves further upstream. [Visual Search Workbench](/projects/visual-search-workbench/) is a local-first macOS product for searching and exploring photo libraries with on-device models. [WorkBay](/projects/workbay/) and its adjacent design R&D treat workflow and interaction structure as durable, versioned state rather than information that has to be reconstructed from a chat transcript or codebase on every pass.

</AboutCard>

<AboutCard label="Practice" title="Heuristics Canon">

I maintain [Heuristics Canon](https://github.com/darce/heuristics-canon), a source-linked set of decision rules I use to review product, interaction, accessibility, AI, and engineering work. The useful part is not the size of the corpus; it is that a design claim can point to a rule, a source, and the artifact that changed because of it. The [research note](/research/heuristics-canon/) describes the instrument.

</AboutCard>

<AboutCard label="Looking for" title="Senior product and design-engineering roles">

I'm most interested in senior product and design-engineering work where ambiguity is real, the interface has to explain complex system behavior, and a prototype is expected to answer product questions rather than decorate them.

</AboutCard>
