## Designing through working software

A working prototype gives a team something to try. A selection leads to another view; that view may expose a choice that was easy to miss in a static screen. Trying the interaction helps us decide what to keep, what to change, and what the implementation needs to support.

I stay involved as those decisions reach production. My front-end work includes React and TypeScript, with accessibility built into the behaviour of the interface. Keyboard interaction and focus management are part of making that behaviour work for the people using it.

## Carrying an idea into production

At [MSNBC](/projects/msnbc/), I helped take a dedicated video experience from product definition through working prototypes to launch in six months. I proposed collapsible panels so viewers could browse coverage and read comments while the video continued playing. Those panels became working front-end interactions, developed with the internal team and external vendors.

At [PhotoShelter](/projects/photoshelter/), my work combined product R&D with accessibility engineering. A workflow-automation prototype exposed integration issues before production development and informed Smart Galleries. Alongside that R&D work, I led a WCAG audit and front-end remediation, including keyboard support across Web Components, and helped other developers adopt accessible patterns.

## Exploring and describing images

My independent image products pursue two related interests: visual exploration and image description.

**Visual exploration** is the focus of [Visual Search Workbench](/projects/visual-search-workbench/), a macOS app I designed and released. A person can start with words or a picture, then follow similar images without tagging the collection first. A map offers another way to browse those relationships. I wanted to leave room for a useful picture to suggest a direction the original query did not anticipate, while keeping processing on the Mac. I have tested the app on an 8 GB machine.

**Image description** is the focus of [AltContext](/projects/altcontext/), which is still in development. It combines a photograph with a publisher’s named people roster and the page’s context to draft a description in WordPress. The editor reviews the identity and wording before applying the text. The intended benefit is more useful context for blind readers, including who is pictured when that matters to the page.

## Keeping design decisions available during development

AI-assisted development introduced another problem into my practice: decisions and review findings could be lost between coding sessions. I built [WorkBay](/projects/workbay/) to preserve those records outside the conversation. A later session can retrieve them, including when I move to another supported coding tool.

Design reviews produce records worth keeping too. I use UX maps to inventory existing interfaces and examine proposed changes. Those maps can be rendered as text screens and flows for people and AI tools to review. [Heuristics Canon](/research/heuristics-canon/), a collection I maintain, supplies specific questions and references for the critique. The resulting findings guide changes that I check in the running interface.
