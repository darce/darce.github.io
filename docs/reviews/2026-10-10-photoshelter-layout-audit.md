# PhotoShelter layout audit and shared portfolio proposal

Reviewed 2026-10-10. Target: [PhotoShelter case study](https://darce.xyz/projects/photoshelter/). Authority: **Heuristics Canon v0.25.6**, as named in the pinned release manifest; all 53 manifested files matched their hashes. Published rows came from that read-only release, with the research repository's distillations and extracted original literature checked separately.

**Correction following owner review:** the first implementation incorrectly capped the whole article at 72ch. At a 1920px viewport, that reduced a 1464px content canvas to about 691px and left about 773px unused on the right. TYPE-01 concerns prose line length; it does not justify constraining every image, diagram, and table to that measure. The original column-wide recommendation is withdrawn. Passing tests established the implemented geometry, not the suitability of that design decision. The proposal is reproduced in the user-facing conversation; printing the file in a tool output did not make it visibly available there.

## Recommendation

Keep the article at the **full available content width**. Use half-width inset figures, alternating right, left, right through the gallery and article, within a continuous narrative. Consecutive paragraphs and subsection headings flow around available images; their position does not identify them as an image explanation. Wrap only when both the desktop layout and actual article container have enough space; stack figures on narrower screens and when enlarged reader text leaves insufficient space. This is a contextual application of the canon, not a numerical prescription from the books.

For PhotoShelter, the static menu image opens on the right, keyboard-navigation GIF sits on the left, and mouse-to-keyboard GIF sits on the right. Captions alone describe each figure. **Correction following the owner's screenshot review:** the earlier image-and-paragraph flow-root groups left empty space and implied that adjacent prose described the image. That grouping recommendation is withdrawn. Figures and paragraphs now share one flow, and subsection headings do not force a new row. Each GIF offers **View full size**, opening a modal that enlarges the image to fit the window and supports further zoom and scrolling. Images retain their full proportions.

At 1440px, the original article was 864px wide, approximately 90 Geist Mono characters per line at 16px. The requested first body GIF occupied the entire 864px and was about 602px tall. With the token-based navigation rail, the corrected article canvas is **984px**, and each inset is **492px**, half that full canvas. At 1920px, the canvas is **1464px**, with **732px** insets. The **72ch** limit (about 691px with this font) remains on summaries and text-only or stacked presentations. In desktop articles containing inset images, paragraph boxes span the full canvas so line boxes can wrap around either side: about 49 characters beside an inset at 1440px and 74 at 1920px. Ordinary prose remains at 16px, with unitless leading of 1.65 rather than a fixed 28.8px.

## Findings and decisions

| Observed condition | Proposed improvement | Canon v0.25.6 basis and source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long desktop lines and large GIFs dominate the narrative. | Keep the full canvas and 1.65 leading. Retain 72ch in text-only and stacked presentations; permit full-width paragraph boxes in a continuous desktop image flow. | **TYPE-01 Measure cap**, **TYPE-02 Readability stool** — Richard Rutter, *Web Typography*, § designing-paragraphs-line-length; contextual exception explained below. |
| A prose measure was incorrectly applied to the entire media and article container. | Separate the reading measure from the full canvas; let supporting figures and complex evidence use the space their roles need. | **LAY-38 Zone by hierarchy** — Timothy Samara, *Making and Breaking the Grid*, ch. 1. |
| Repeated full-width screenshots dominate the first section. | Use alternating half-width insets for these compact demonstrations; retain a wide option for complex evidence. | **LAY-38 Zone by hierarchy**, **LAY-44 Storyboard the cadence** — Timothy Samara, *Making and Breaking the Grid*, ch. 1. |
| A flow-root group containing an image and one paragraph creates a false association and leaves a gap under the paragraph. | Remove those groups; allow consecutive paragraphs and subsection headings to continue around alternating figures. Captions remain figure-specific; DOM order stays linear. | **LAY-37 Protect manuscript continuity** — Samara, ch. 1; **PERC-03 Seven-principle Gestalt audit** — Jeff Johnson, *Designing with the Mind in Mind*. |
| Opening a 640px GIF in a new tab still displays a small image. | Use a window-filling native modal with fit, zoom, scroll, Close, Escape, contained keyboard focus, and restored opener focus. | **A11Y-11 Keyboard walk**, **A11Y-12 Native first, ARIA last** — WCAG 2.2; [WAI-ARIA modal dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/). |
| A desktop breakpoint alone does not guarantee usable wrap width. | Require a 42rem article container as well as the desktop layout. Stack below this threshold and allow reader enlargement to trigger reflow. | **TYPE-13 Em-based type response** — Rutter, § responsive-paragraphs; **LAY-42 Build outward from mobile** — Samara, ch. 1; **A11Y-08 Reflow and resize (1.4.4/1.4.10/1.4.12)** — WCAG 2.2, SC 1.4.4. |
| Captions lack a shared semantic role, and heading spacing does little to mark section transitions. | Use figure/figcaption, 0.875rem captions with 1.5 leading and full opacity, plus 48px above / 12px below subsection headings. | **PERC-09 Hierarchy for scanners** — Steve Krug, *Don't Make Me Think, Revisited*, ch. 3; **LAY-38 Zone by hierarchy** — Samara, ch. 1. |
| A minimum 450px / 35% navigation rail competes with the reading column. | Use a shared rail minimum, viewport share, and maximum; let the remaining track shrink with `minmax(0, 1fr)`. | **LAY-36 Inventory before closure**, **LAY-38 Zone by hierarchy** — Samara, ch. 1. |

The temporary prose strip beside an inset is about 33 characters wide at the minimum wrapping size. The 42rem threshold and wide option prevent unusably narrow wrapping. Alternation supplies cadence; a complex chart, detailed interface comparison, or essential small labels can still warrant a wide figure. Cropping or blindly shrinking every image would defeat the evidence.

**TYPE-01 contextual exception:** an isolated 72ch paragraph box obstructs left-side wrapping: at 1920px the left figure itself is 732px wide, larger than that box. Continuous media-bearing prose therefore uses the full canvas. Lines below floats can exceed 75 characters, a readability tradeoff accepted to implement the owner's explicit request for uninterrupted flow. This does not invalidate the reading-range heuristic or establish an empirically superior reading speed. Summaries and stacked/text-only articles retain the measure cap.

Rutter's original discusses the 45–75-character range as accumulated practice and qualifies it with screen studies: longer lines can be read faster while shorter lines can be preferred. Printed p. 25 demonstrates a maximum width on the paragraph itself. His approximate 38em assumes a different average character width. For this monospaced face, 72ch expresses the intended character count directly; it is a deliberate translation rather than a literal adoption of 38em. Neither this source nor the retained layout rules mandate a universal figure ratio or a smaller article canvas. These choices still need reader observation to establish a preference, beyond geometric and accessibility checks. On exceptionally wide displays, the prose beside a half-width figure can exceed the usual reading range; that remains a contextual tradeoff of retaining the full canvas and the requested half-width relationship.

## Shared design tokens and propagation

The source is [`design-tokens/portfolio.tokens.json`](../../design-tokens/portfolio.tokens.json), using the [DTCG Format Module 2025.10](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/2025.10/format/). Dimensions use the permitted `px`/`rem` object representation. Ratios, character counts, and unitless leading are number tokens. Group type inheritance and a gutter alias preserve the relationship to shared spacing.

| Design decision | Token | Value / web interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Navigation rail | `layout.rail.minimum`, `viewport-share`, `maximum` | 18rem / 0.28 × 100vw / 24rem inside `clamp()` |
| Prose width | `article.measure-characters` | 72 × 1ch in summaries and text-only/stacked presentations; continuous desktop inset flow uses the full canvas |
| Insets | `article.inset.minimum-container`, `width-share`, `gutter` | 42rem / 0.5 × full canvas width / alias of `spacing.large` |
| Body and caption leading | `typography.body.line-height`, `typography.caption.line-height` | 1.65 / 1.5 |
| Caption size | `typography.caption.size` | 0.875rem |
| Image viewer | `article.viewer.edge`, `padding`, `control-size`, `zoom-factor` | spacing aliases / 2.75rem controls / 2 × fitted size |

The web exporter generates Sass before development, checking, testing, and building. Existing Sass spacing, breakpoint, and typography names delegate to this source, so consumers keep a stable API. Browser assertions consume the same JSON. Image `sizes="auto, 100vw"` follows the actual rendered size where supported, with a conservative viewport fallback rather than one derived from prose length. Palette and motion tokens retain their existing sources; this is a bounded migration, not a claim that every portfolio token has been converted.

The shared `ProjectDetails` renderer applies the contextual paragraph cap, full article canvas, continuous inset flow, title and section rhythm, caption treatment, and alternating gallery figures to project and research detail pages. MDX authors can use self-closing `Figure` among ordinary narrative paragraphs, opt into `layout="wide"` for the full canvas, override `side` for a specific placement, or enable the shared modal with `inspectable`. Legacy nested figure children become siblings in the same flow. Existing diagrams, paired comparisons, and ASCII drawings retain their own structures. Text exports retain the image, caption, and prose when JSX tags are removed.

## Verification

### Earlier canvas correction (09d8bb0)

The correction's failing browser case reproduced the published width regression: **691.1875px actual versus 1464px available** at a 1920px viewport. Revised shared checks require the article to fill its available canvas, keep standalone paragraphs within the reading range, and place the right-side inset at the actual canvas edge. They cover PhotoShelter, MSNBC, and WorkBay. Existing checks retain actual text-line geometry alongside both alternating GIFs, correct figure widths and sides, loaded evidence, and headings below their figure groups. The reflow matrix covers 320, 768, 1024, 1440, and 1920px with enlarged root text and user text-spacing overrides.

The first publication passed `npm run check`, 136 unit tests, and 48 browser checks, but its shared measure test asserted a narrow article container. Those results did not validate the mistaken canvas reduction. The correction passes `npm run check` (no errors; six existing warnings), **136 unit tests in 22 files**, the complete production export, and **48 browser checks**, including both themes, accessibility, navigation, image loading, source metadata, and intact ASCII drawings. Visual review covered 390px mobile, 1440px desktop in both themes, and 1920px desktop. Measured canvases and insets are 984px / 492px at 1440px, 1464px / 732px at 1920px, and 342px / 342px stacked at 390px. The deployed commit is recorded with the correction's publication receipt.

### Continuous narrative and modal correction

The new failing checks reproduced the owner's screenshot: the next paragraph began at y478.2, below the preceding figure's bottom at y427.2, and the modal-opening button was absent because inspection still used a new-tab link. Removing the flow-root wrappers and subsection clearance lets real text lines continue beside the preceding GIF. Checks at 1440px and 1920px confirm continuity, right/left/right alternation, the original full canvases (984px and 1464px), and half-width figures (492px and 732px). At 390px the 342px figures stack, with no page overflow.

The modal enlarges the first GIF from 492px inline to about **1,283px painted width** in a 1440 × 1000 window. Zoom doubles the fitted image itself, rather than doubling a letterboxed container; the mobile zoom image is 680 × 474px in a 340px-wide viewport and starts at the scrolling origin. It preserves animation and proportions, stays on the same page, and supports fit, zoom, pointer/keyboard scrolling, Close, and Escape. Browser testing caught native Tab moving focus outside the dialog; explicit first/last-control handling now cycles Tab and Shift+Tab inside it. Background focus is denied, opening-button focus and body scrolling are restored on close, and image swipes do not activate sibling-page navigation.

The final implementation passes type checks and lint with no new warnings, **136 unit tests**, a complete production export, and **51 browser checks**. The modal checks cover mobile, tablet, and desktop, both themes, both GIFs, and 200% reader text. The article reflow matrix retains 320–1920px coverage with enlarged text and spacing overrides. Visual inspection covered continuous desktop flow and light/dark modal fit, plus mobile fit and zoom. These checks establish the requested geometry and interaction; they do not establish a reader-preference study or a complete assistive-technology audit. The publication receipt records the deployed commit and live verification.
